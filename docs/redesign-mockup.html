Design response · 23 August 2026

Hierarchy, contrast, and the report screen.

A redesign pass against the review. It deliberately changes nothing decorative: the palette, the typefaces and the warmth stay. What changes is what the interface emphasises, how readable the quiet text is, and the order in which a frightened person meets the information in their own report.

secondary text 4.26 → 5.49 body type 13 → 16px cards on Home 8 → 3 tap targets ≥44pt

The measurement that settles the colour argument

The review suspected the muted text failed contrast. It does, and two semantic colours fail worse. These are computed against the canvas #F2E9DA, which is what most secondary text actually sits on.

What this pass deliberately did not do

The review's closing instruction was to spend the next pass on hierarchy, accessibility and report UX rather than decoration, so:

No new palette
The warm neutrals stay. Only the muted values move, and only far enough to pass.
No new typefaces
Same Nunito pairing. The scale changes, not the voice.
No shadows, radii or ornament touched
Density now comes from grouping rather than from shrinking the text.

Two items from the review are product decisions rather than design ones, and are left open: whether vaccine recommendations should genuinely personalise on medical history, and whether medication frequency becomes a structured field. Both change what the app stores, not how it looks.
