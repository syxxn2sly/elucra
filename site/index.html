How it reads a document

Three passes, because one would be guessing.

A single model call that reads a page and explains it in one go can invent a number and sound just as fluent doing it. Clairity separates reading from explaining, then checks the explanation back against what was read.

01

Read

The page is transcribed into facts, each one tagged with where on the sheet it appeared. Nothing is interpreted yet. Anything genuinely illegible is marked unknown rather than guessed.

02

Explain

A second pass receives only those facts, never the image. It can rewrite them in plain language, and it has nothing else to draw on, so there is no outside medical knowledge for it to add.

03

Check

Your phone then compares the explanation against the facts. A figure that does not appear on the page, or a warning that traces to nothing, is removed before you ever see it.

What it holds

The paperwork of being a patient, in one place.

Explaining one document helps once. Most of what makes appointments stressful is not having the rest of it to hand.

A summary for your doctor

Medications, recent results and the questions you meant to ask, gathered into something you can hold up in the room.

Things to say

Scripts for the calls people put off, each labelled by what it is for: asking the clinic when your appointment is, asking a pharmacist about a side effect.

Medications

Doses and schedules, with names normalised against a drug reference so the same medicine written two ways is still one medicine.

Vaccines

What you have had and what is outstanding, including multi-dose courses that are easy to lose track of halfway through.

Readings you take at home

Blood pressure, glucose, oxygen, weight and temperature, dated when you took them, with the reference figures shown beside them.

Screening that fits you

What is due is filtered by age, province and the conditions on your profile, rather than a general list you have to sort through yourself.

Your health card

Photographed and kept behind Face ID, for the moment at a desk when you need the number and cannot find the card.

Appointments

What is coming and what has been, with the paperwork from each visit attached to the visit it came from.

Sixteen languages

Translated after the checking, not before.

The English explanation is verified first, then translated. Doing it the other way round would hand every safety check a mistranslated page to validate.

Numbers pass through untouched

Doses, strengths, dates and measurements are carried across exactly as written, in the same digits and the same units. A dosage is the one thing a translation must never round or reformat.

The English stays beside it

For instructions and warnings, both versions are shown together. A dropped "do not" reads perfectly fluently in any language, and keeping the original in view is the honest way to handle that.

Privacy

Your record lives on your phone.

Clairity has no account to sign into and no server holding your history. What you enter and what you save stays in the app's own storage on your device.

What leaves the device, and when

One thing does: when you ask for a document to be explained, that page is sent to Anthropic's Claude API to be read, through a small service that holds the API credentials so they are never inside the app. Nothing else goes with it. Your other records, your health card and your profile are not uploaded.

Deleting it means deleting it

Because the record is only on this phone, removing the app removes the data, and there is no copy elsewhere to ask for. That cuts both ways, which is why the app offers an export file before it offers the button that erases everything.

Your health card

Stored encrypted and opened with Face ID, Touch ID, or your device passcode. It is never sent anywhere.

What it is not

Clairity explains your document. It does not practise medicine.

  • It does not diagnose, and it does not tell you whether a result is good or bad news.
  • It will not suggest starting, stopping or changing a medication.
  • Software reading a page can misread it. Check anything that matters against the original, especially doses and numbers.
  • It is not a substitute for your doctor, nurse or pharmacist, and it is not for emergencies. If something is urgent, call your local emergency number.

Support

Something wrong, or something missing?

Clairity is a small, independent project. Mail is read by the person who builds it, and a note about what you were doing when it went wrong is genuinely the most useful thing you can send.
